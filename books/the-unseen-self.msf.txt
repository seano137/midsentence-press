§1 EGYPT_AND_SCRIPTURE
c1.1 [p=0.90] The Egyptians held the heart, not the brain, to be the seat of thought, memory and moral judgment, weighed against the feather of Maat after death.
c1.2 [p=0.85] The Egyptian person was understood as plural: body (khat), vital double (ka), mobile soul (ba), transfigured spirit (akh), true name (ren) and shadow, each requiring its own ritual care.
c1.3 [p=0.80] The Hebrew scriptures and the non-canonical Enoch literature describe a populated, ranked invisible hierarchy of angels, demons and watchers with direct bearing on human fate.
?q1.1 Whether the Dead Sea Scrolls' "two spirits" doctrine is a genuine Essene innovation or an older idea first attested there.
!disagree

§2 EAST_AND_WEST
c2.1 [p=0.90] The Upanishadic claim that the individual self (Ātman) is identical with the absolute (Brahman) treats the waking ego as a temporary limitation on a deeper, unchanging awareness. ~c1.2
c2.2 [p=0.85] Yogic and Tantric systems map a subtle body of channels (nāḍīs), centres (cakras) and a coiled force (kuṇḍalinī) as the operative anatomy beneath ordinary consciousness.
c2.3 [p=0.75] Chinese Taoist and Japanese esoteric traditions independently describe a refinement of the body's vital substance toward a more continuous, luminous awareness, without contact with Indian sources.
c2.4 [p=0.85] Hermetic, Kabbalistic and Sufi systems each hold that the human being mirrors the cosmos (microcosm/macrocosm) and that a trained inner faculty (nous, the sefirot's pattern, the latāʾif) can ascend beyond the ordinary self. ~c2.1
c2.5 [p=0.70] Freemasonry and related societies preserve, in symbolic and ritual form, the same death-and-rebirth structure found in Egyptian initiation and the Hermetic ascent. ~c1.2

§3 PHYSICS_AND_MAGIC
c3.1 [p=0.80] Pre-1950 physics (Faraday, Maxwell, the luminiferous ether) and older occult philosophy both posit a subtle intermediate medium between dense matter and pure force or spirit.
c3.2 [p=0.75] Tesla's own writings describe the earth and atmosphere as a living, chargeable conductor and propose a universal energy continuum tappable by engineered means, in language continuous with the older "ether" and "astral light."
c3.3 [p=0.70] Alchemy's operations on metal (blackening, whitening, reddening, the stone's birth) were read by their own practitioners, and later by Jung, as stages of psychological transformation as much as material ones.
c3.4 [p=0.65] Ceremonial magic's effectiveness, where claimed, is attributed by its own texts to the purity and concentration of the operator's consciousness rather than to the ritual apparatus alone.

§4 MIND_BENEATH_MIND
c4.1 [p=0.90] Mesmer's and later the hypnotists' clinical work established that a region of mind outside ordinary waking awareness exists and can be influenced by suggestion or by another operator.
c4.2 [p=0.85] Freud's unconscious (repression, dreams as "the royal road") and Jung's collective unconscious (archetypes shared across unrelated cultures) both describe a stratum beneath the surface personality that shapes it without being directly known by it. ~c2.1 ~c1.1
c4.3 [p=0.75] Early-twentieth-century propaganda technique deliberately applied the same mechanisms of suggestion and unconscious appeal that the clinic had identified, which the book treats as practical confirmation that the targeted layer of mind is real.

§5 CONVERGENCE_AND_CONCLUSION
c5.1 [p=0.80] Egypt, India, the Hebrew apocalyptic tradition, the Western esoteric line and early depth psychology each independently describe: a multiple/layered self, a centre near the heart, channels or vehicles carrying a subtle force, a populated unseen world, and a trainable path from ordinary to clearer awareness. ~c1.1 ~c1.2 ~c2.1 ~c2.2 ~c2.4 ~c4.2
?q5.1 Whether independent convergence across unconnected cultures is best explained by a shared underlying reality, by common features of human neurology and experience, or by coincidence — the book argues for the first and states this as its own view, not a settled finding.
c5.2 [p=0.60] The author's stated conclusion: human consciousness is a local, temporary focusing of a more universal awareness mediated by graded vehicles of which the physical body is the densest — offered explicitly as the author's own reading, not as proof.
!disagree
